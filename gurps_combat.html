Combat Systems and Tactical Play: When Dice Meet Steel

From First Blood to Final Victory

The Theater of Violence

Combat in GURPS is like conducting a deadly orchestra—every participant has their moment to act, timing is everything, and one wrong move can end the performance permanently. Unlike many game systems that treat combat as a mini-game separate from the rest of play, GURPS combat uses the same core mechanics you've already learned, but adds layers of tactical depth that reward smart thinking and realistic tactics.

The Chess Match Analogy

Think of GURPS combat like speed chess played with real consequences. Each turn represents one second of intense action where you must choose your move carefully—attack aggressively, defend cautiously, maneuver for advantage, or attempt something unexpected. Like chess, every piece (character) has different capabilities, and victory often goes to the player who thinks several moves ahead rather than the one with the most powerful pieces.

The Dance of Battle: Combat Sequence

GURPS combat flows in a structured sequence that simulates the chaos of real fighting while keeping gameplay organized and fair:

Initiative: Who Goes First?

Basic Speed determines your place in the action sequence, like your position in a relay race. Higher Basic Speed means you react faster to danger and opportunities. Ties go to the higher DX; if that's tied too, roll a die.

Initiative Example: The Warehouse Confrontation

Our heroes encounter armed criminals in an abandoned warehouse:

Character Basic Speed Combat Position
Jack "Ace" Morrison (Action Hero) 6.25 Goes first - military training pays off
Detective Tre 6.0 Second - police training
Criminal Leader 5.75 Third - experienced but older
Dr. Rita (Archaeologist) 5.25 Fourth - not built for combat
Thug #1 5.0 Fifth - muscle, not brains

The One-Second Turn

Each combat turn represents one second of real time. On your turn you choose one maneuver; the most common are:

  • Attack: Strike with weapon or fist
  • Move: Run up to Basic Move in yards
  • Move and Attack: Move and strike in the same turn, at a penalty
  • Aim: Steady a ranged weapon to gain its Accuracy bonus
  • Concentrate: Cast a spell or focus on a mental task
  • Ready: Draw a weapon, reload, or prepare equipment
  • All-Out Attack: Aggressive assault, trading defense for offense
  • All-Out Defense: Pure defense, giving up attacks for protection
  • Others: Feint, Evaluate, Change Posture, Wait, and Do Nothing

The Art of Attack: Landing the Blow

Attack Rolls: Skill vs. Reality

Attacking in GURPS uses the same 3d6 ≤ target number system you've learned, but the target number comes from your weapon skill modified by circumstances:

Base Target Number = Weapon Skill
+ Range Modifier (ranged attacks: 0 out to 2 yards, growing penalty beyond)
+ Size Modifier (larger targets easier to hit)
+ Hit Location (torso 0, everything else a penalty)
+ Aiming (the weapon's Accuracy bonus if you took an Aim maneuver)
+ Circumstance Modifier (posture, cover, lighting, distractions)
= Final Attack Target Number

Range and Accuracy

GURPS uses realistic range modifiers—there's no bonus for being close, and the penalty grows steadily with distance, so even skilled shooters struggle at long range:

Size Matters: Target Size Modifiers

Bigger targets are easier to hit, smaller ones harder—common sense made mechanical:

Human-Sized Targets (Size 0)

Average adults, most equipment

Modifier: 0

Large Targets (+2 to +4)

Cars, small buildings, giants

Example: Shooting at a car gets roughly +3 or +4, depending on its size

Small Targets (-2 to -4)

Children (about -1), small animals; aiming at a body part uses a similar hit-location penalty

Example: Targeting someone's hand gets -4

Tiny Targets (-6 or worse)

Precise shots, small mechanisms

Example: Shooting the lock off a door might be -5 or worse (the GM's call)

Complete Attack Example

Jack fires his pistol at the criminal leader across the warehouse:

  • Base Skill: Guns (Pistol)-15
  • Range: 20 yards = -6 penalty
  • Target Size: Human = 0
  • Cover: Target half-hidden behind a crate = -2 (an illustrative GM call)
  • Aiming: Took the previous turn to Aim = +2 (the pistol's Accuracy)

Final Target: 15 - 6 + 0 - 2 + 2 = 9

Roll: Jack rolls 8—success by 1! Unless the leader makes his Dodge, the bullet hits.

The Shield and the Sword: Active Defenses

GURPS combat shines in its defense system. When attacked, defenders don't just stand there—they actively try to avoid harm through dodge, block, or parry. It's like a conversation where every statement gets a response.

Three Pillars of Defense

Dodge (Basic Speed-based)

Getting out of the way entirely

Base Value: Basic Speed + 3 (drop fractions)

Best Against: Ranged attacks, area effects, when unarmed

Limitations: Can't dodge if you don't see the attack coming

Example: Tre (Basic Speed 6.0) has Dodge 9 (6 + 3). When the thug swings a crowbar at her, she tries to dodge—rolls 7, success! She ducks under the swing.

Block (Shield-based)

Using a shield (or cloak) to intercept attacks

Base Value: 3 + half your Shield skill (drop fractions)

Best Against: Melee attacks, arrows and thrown weapons (not bullets)

Limitations: Need appropriate equipment, limited arc of protection

Example: A medieval knight with Shield-14 has Block 10 (3 + 7), and his medium shield's Defense Bonus of +2 raises it to 12. He raises his shield against an arrow—rolls 11, success! The arrow embeds harmlessly in the shield.

Parry (Weapon skill-based)

Using a weapon to deflect incoming attacks

Base Value: 3 + half your weapon skill (drop fractions)

Best Against: Melee attacks, some projectiles

Limitations: Need appropriate weapon, some attacks can't be parried

Example: A fencer with Rapier-16 has Parry 11 (3 + 8). Against a sword thrust, she rolls 10—success! She deflects the blade with a clever twist of her wrist.

Choosing Your Defense

Smart fighters match their defense to the situation:

Situation Best Defense Reasoning
Sniper shot from unknown direction None possible No active defense against an attack you don't know is coming; only cover and DR help
Sword duel with expert fencer Parry Use weapon skill, maintain attacking position
Multiple attackers surrounding you Dodge Dodge has no penalty for repeated use; extra parries with one weapon take -4 each, and you get one Block per turn
Arrows while carrying a shield Block Shields can block arrows and thrown weapons (not bullets)
Grenade explosion Dodge (dive for cover) Can't block or parry a blast; getting distance or cover is what helps

Defense Penalties and Limitations

Defending isn't always easy or possible:

  • Multiple Attacks: You can Dodge any number of times, but each parry after the first with the same weapon is at a cumulative -4 (-2 for fencing weapons), and you can Block only once per turn
  • Surprise: Can't defend if you don't know the attack is coming
  • Flanks and rear: -2 to defend against attacks from the side; no active defense against attacks from behind
  • Retreat: Give ground (once per turn) to get +3 to Dodge, +1 to Parry or Block (+3 for parries with fencing weapons, Boxing, Judo, or Karate)
  • All-Out Attack: Trade all defenses for better attacks
  • Stunned: -4 to all active defenses until you recover

When Steel Meets Flesh: Damage and Injury

GURPS damage isn't just hit points—it's a simulation of realistic injury that affects performance. Think of it like a car taking damage: minor dents might not affect performance, but major damage starts limiting what the engine can do.

Types of Damage

graph TD A[Damage Types] --> B[Crushing] A --> C[Cutting] A --> D[Impaling] A --> E[Piercing] A --> F[Burning] A --> G[Toxic] B --> B1[Blunt impacts<br/>Clubs, falls, explosions] B --> B2[1x damage to HP] C --> C1[Sharp edges<br/>Swords, axes, claws] C --> C2[1.5x damage to HP] D --> D1[Deep penetration<br/>Spears, stakes, arrows] D --> D2[2x damage to HP] E --> E1[Small holes<br/>Bullets, needles] E --> E2[x0.5 to x2<br/>based on size] F --> F1[Heat and energy<br/>Fire, lasers, electricity] F --> F2[1x damage, ongoing effects] G --> G1[Poison and disease<br/>Venom, radiation] G --> G2[1x damage, often<br/>resisted with HT]

Damage Calculation

Muscle-powered weapons get their damage from your ST: at ST 10 your thrust is 1d-2 and your swing is 1d, and a weapon is listed relative to those (a broadsword is "sw+1 cutting"). When an attack connects, you roll damage, subtract the target's DR, and only then apply the wounding multiplier for the damage type:

Damage Example: The Knife Slash

Jack (ST 12, swing 1d+2) slashes the criminal leader with a large knife (sw-2, so 1d cutting):

  1. Roll basic damage: Rolls 5 = 5 points
  2. Subtract armor: Leather jacket DR 1 stops 1 point = 4 points get through
  3. Apply damage type: Cutting × 1.5 = 6 points of injury
  4. Apply to HP: Criminal had 12 HP, now has 6 HP

The criminal is now at exactly half HP—he's hurt but still dangerous. Six points isn't more than half his HP, so it isn't a major wound, but shock gives him -4 (the maximum) to DX and IQ on his next turn.

Injury Effects: The Death Spiral

GURPS doesn't pile up a steady penalty for every point lost. Instead, injury bites at a few key thresholds:

Shock and Stunning

Any injury rattles you briefly, and big hits can temporarily incapacitate even tough characters:

Shock Penalties

Every point of injury gives -1 to DX, IQ, and skills based on them on your next turn only, to a maximum of -4. Like getting the wind knocked out of you.

A major wound—a single hit that inflicts more than half your HP—is worse: make a HT roll or be knocked down and stunned (fail by 5 or more and you're knocked out). While stunned you can only Do Nothing, your active defenses are at -4, and you roll HT at the end of each turn to shake it off.

Example: Rita (9 HP) takes 8 points from a club hit. That's more than half her HP (4.5), so it's a major wound and she must roll vs HT 10. She rolls 13—failure by 3! Rita is knocked down and stunned, and with just 1 HP left (under 1/3 of her HP) her Move and Dodge are halved even after she recovers.

Death and Dying

GURPS characters don't just drop dead at 0 HP—they fight to survive:

  • Less than 1/3 HP left: Reeling; halve your Move and Dodge
  • 0 HP or less: Roll vs HT every turn you act; fail and you fall unconscious
  • -1×HP: Make a HT roll or die, and again at -2×HP, -3×HP, and -4×HP
  • -5×HP: Automatic death (at -10×HP the body is destroyed)
  • Medical care: First Aid or Surgery can stabilize and treat the wounded

Beyond Hack and Slash: Advanced Combat Options

GURPS combat offers rich tactical choices beyond "I attack the monster." Like a chess master, experienced players use position, timing, and clever tactics to overcome stronger opponents.

Maneuvers and Special Attacks

All-Out Attack

Trade: All defenses for one option: Determined (+4 to hit in melee, +1 at range), Strong (+2 damage or +1 per die, whichever is better), Double (two melee attacks), or Feint then attack

Use When: Overwhelming weak enemies, finishing moves

Risk: Vulnerable to counterattacks

All-Out Defense

Trade: All attacks for Increased Defense (+2 to one defense: Dodge, Parry, or Block) or Double Defense (try a second, different defense if the first fails)

Use When: Outnumbered, waiting for help, protecting others

Benefit: Very hard to hit

Aim

Trade: This turn's action for accuracy next turn

Benefit: The weapon's Accuracy after one turn, +1 more for a second turn, +2 for three or more

Use When: Long-range shots, precision strikes

Move and Attack

Trade: Full attack bonus for mobility

Penalty: Melee at -4 (effective skill no better than 9); ranged at -2 or the weapon's Bulk, whichever is worse; no retreating

Use When: Charging, hit-and-run tactics

Feint

Contest: Quick Contest of your melee skill vs the opponent's best melee skill (or Shield/Cloak)

Benefit: Your margin of victory comes off their defense against your next attack

Use When: Fighting defensive enemies

Grappling

Contest: Grab with DX (or Judo/Wrestling) as an attack; breaking free is a Quick Contest of ST

Benefit: Control opponent's actions

Use When: Subduing without killing, disarming

Targeting Body Parts

Skilled fighters can target specific areas for tactical advantage:

Target Penalty Special Effects Tactical Use
Skull -7 Extra DR 2, ×4 injury, knockdown risk Instant takedown attempts
Vitals -3 ×3 injury, impaling and piercing attacks only Maximum damage efficiency
Arms -2 Cripple limb on injury over 1/2 HP, drop weapons Disarming, limiting attacks
Legs -2 Cripple limb on injury over 1/2 HP, reduce mobility Preventing escape/pursuit
Hands -4 Cripple on injury over 1/3 HP, drop held items Precise disarming
Eyes -9 ×4 injury (impaling, piercing, beams), blindness Desperation moves only

Environmental Tactics

Smart fighters use their surroundings as weapons:

Positioning and Terrain

  • High Ground: Fighting from above gives an edge (the Basic Set has rules for combat at different levels)
  • Flanking: Defenders are at -2 against attacks from the side and get no active defense from the rear
  • Cover: Walls, trees, cars hide hit locations and add their DR
  • Chokepoints: Narrow passages limit enemy numbers
  • Darkness: Hide your approach, confuse enemies

Improvised Weapons

  • Chairs, bottles, tools: Crushing damage based on your ST, often with a penalty to hit (GM's call)
  • Thrown objects: Distraction or minor damage
  • Environmental hazards: Push enemies into dangers
  • Blinding attacks: Throw sand, use flashlights

Putting It All Together: Complete Combat Examples

Example One: The Alley Ambush

Detective Tre investigates suspicious activity behind a warehouse. Two thugs with crowbars try to ambush her. (Illustrative stats: Tre has Basic Speed 6.0, HP 11, HT 12, Dodge 9, Guns-12; each thug has ST 13, HP 12, and swings his crowbar at Axe/Mace-11 for 2d crushing.)

Turn 1: Initiative and Opening Moves

Tre (Speed 6.0) - Goes First

Action: Draw pistol (Ready maneuver)

Position: Back to alley wall, good defensive position

Result: Weapon ready, can defend normally

Thug #1 (Speed 5.0)

Action: All-Out Attack (Determined) with crowbar

Target Number: Axe/Mace-11 +4 (All-Out) = 15

Roll: 12 - Success by 3!

Tre's Defense: Can't parry (a pistol is no melee weapon), chooses Dodge 9

Defense Roll: 10 - Failure by 1!

Damage: 2d crushing = rolls 9 points, no armor, ×1 for crushing = 9 injury!

Effect: Tre drops from 11 HP to 2 HP. Nine points is more than half her HP, so it's a major wound: she must roll vs HT to stay on her feet

HT Roll: Rolls 11 vs HT 12 - Success! She isn't stunned or knocked down, but she's badly hurt: shock gives her -4 to DX next turn, and under 1/3 HP her Move and Dodge are halved

Thug #2 (Speed 5.0)

Action: Move to flank Tre

Result: Now attacking from her side, so she'll defend at -2 next turn

Turn 2: Desperate Measures

Tre (at 2 HP, -4 shock, Dodge halved to 5)

Action: All-Out Attack (Determined) - one shot at Thug #1, point-blank

Shot: Guns-12 +1 (All-Out, ranged) -4 (shock) = 9, rolls 8 - Success!

Thug's Defense: He made an All-Out Attack last turn, so he gets no defense

Damage to Thug #1: 2d+2 piercing (9mm) = rolls 10+2 = 12, ×1 for piercing = 12 injury. He drops to 0 HP, fails his major-wound HT roll, and goes down!

Thug #2 (flanking position)

Action: Attack Tre (she can't defend at all after an All-Out Attack)

Target: Axe/Mace-11 (flanking doesn't add to hit; it only costs the defender -2, which doesn't matter now) = 11, rolls 8 - Success!

Damage: 2d crushing = 9 points

Result: Tre drops to -7 HP. Below 0 she must roll HT each turn to stay conscious; she fails and collapses. Four more points (to -11, her -1×HP) and she'd be rolling HT to avoid death.

Aftermath: Consequences of Violence

Tre's aggressive tactic worked partially—she eliminated one threat—but the cost was severe. She's unconscious at -7 HP, helpless if the second thug keeps swinging, and facing a long recovery even with First Aid. This demonstrates GURPS combat's deadliness and the importance of tactical thinking over pure aggression.

Example Two: The Tactical Approach

Jack encounters the same scenario but uses different tactics:

Jack's Approach: Mobility and Cover

Turn 1: Assessment and Positioning

Jack's Action: Move to cover behind a dumpster, pistol already in hand thanks to a successful Fast-Draw roll

Thug Reactions: Must advance into open to reach Jack

Result: Jack forces enemies to come to him on his terms

Turn 2: Aimed Fire

Jack's Action: Aim at closest thug

Thugs: Advance but still not in melee range

Result: +2 (the pistol's Accuracy) to next attack, enemies must expose themselves

Turn 3: Precise Elimination

Jack's Shot: Guns-15 +2 (Aim) -2 (5 yards) = 15, rolls 9 - Success by 6! The thug, caught in the open, fails his Dodge

Damage: 2d+2 piercing = rolls 8+2 = 10, ×1 for piercing = 10 injury. That's a major wound on a 12-HP thug; he fails his HT roll and is knocked down and stunned

Second Thug: Attacks, but Jack defends normally (no All-Out Attack)

Result: Jack Dodges (Basic Speed 6.25 + 3 = Dodge 9) and keeps controlling the fight

Tactical Takeaways

  • Patience pays: Taking time to aim dramatically improved hit chances
  • Cover matters: Positioning forced enemies to fight on Jack's terms
  • Balanced approach: Not using All-Out Attack preserved defensive options
  • Range advantage: Using a ranged weapon against melee attackers

Tools of War: Armor and Equipment

Armor: The Last Line of Defense

Armor in GURPS provides Damage Resistance (DR)—it subtracts directly from incoming damage, before any wounding multiplier. Think of it like the crumple zone in a car: it absorbs impact before it reaches you. Armor doesn't lower your DX; its weight counts toward encumbrance, which reduces Move and Dodge. The DR values below are illustrative ballparks—use the armor tables in the Basic Set or your genre book for real stats.

Armor Type DR Coverage Drawbacks Best Against
Leather Jacket 1 Torso, arms None Minor cuts, abrasions
Kevlar Vest 4 Torso only Hot to wear for long Bullets, stabbing
Medieval Mail 4 Torso, arms Heavy, noisy; weaker vs crushing Cutting attacks
Plate Armor 7 Full body Very heavy (encumbrance) All medieval weapons
Riot Gear 3* Full body Bulky; helmet limits vision Blunt impacts, chemicals

*Riot gear has special properties against different damage types

Weapons: Extensions of Intent

Weapons in GURPS have distinct characteristics that affect tactical choices:

Melee Weapons

  • Reach: How far you can strike (C for close combat, 1 yard, or more for polearms)
  • Parry Modifier: Some weapons parry better than others
  • Damage Type: Cutting, crushing, or impaling
  • Requirements: Minimum ST to use effectively
  • Damage: Based on your thrust or swing (e.g., sw+1), so stronger fighters hit harder

Large knife: sw-2 cutting or thr impaling (1d-2 either way at ST 10), reach C-1, easy to conceal

Broadsword: sw+1 cutting (1d+1 at ST 10), reach 1, balanced offense/defense

Spear: thr+2 impaling (1d at ST 10), reach 1 (reach 1-2 used two-handed), excellent vs charges

Ranged Weapons

  • Accuracy: Bonus when you Aim
  • Range: Effective distance brackets
  • Rate of Fire: How many shots per turn
  • Recoil: How well rapid-fire shots stay on target

Pistol (9mm): 2d+2 piercing, easy to conceal, moderate range

Battle rifle: around 7d piercing, high accuracy, long range

Shotgun: Variable damage, devastating close range

Equipment Quality and Modification

Not all equipment is created equal:

Quality Levels

  • Cheap: Unreliable, breaks easily
  • Good: Standard quality
  • Fine: Well-made, reliable, less likely to break
  • Very Fine: Masterwork quality

Example: A master gunsmith's custom pistol might be Fine (accurate), adding to its Accuracy when you Aim—the difference between missing and hitting in critical moments.

  • Scenario B: Single opponent with higher skill but you have ranged weapon advantage
  • Scenario C: Need to subdue someone without killing them
  • Scenario D: Protecting a non-combatant while under fire

Activity 3: Damage Calculation Practice

Calculate final damage for these attacks:

  • Broadsword (3d6 cutting) rolls 14 damage against chainmail (DR 4)

Combat Philosophy: Making Violence Matter

When to Fight, When to Flee

GURPS combat is deadly enough that smart characters avoid unnecessary fights. Combat should feel like a serious decision, not the default solution to every problem.

Alternatives to Combat

  • Negotiation: Talk your way out of trouble
  • Stealth: Avoid the fight entirely
  • Intimidation: Make enemies back down
  • Distraction: Create opportunities to escape
  • Environmental solutions: Use surroundings to your advantage

Combat as Character Development

How characters fight reveals who they are:

  • The Professional: Efficient, precise, no wasted motion
  • The Berserker: All-out aggression, damn the consequences
  • The Protector: Defensive, shields others from harm
  • The Tactician: Uses environment and positioning
  • The Survivor: Fights dirty, uses any advantage

Consequences and Recovery

GURPS combat has lasting consequences that affect future adventures:

  • Physical injuries: Take time to heal, affect performance
  • Mental trauma: Witnessing or causing violence affects psychology
  • Social consequences: Violence often attracts unwanted attention
  • Equipment damage: Weapons and armor wear out, break
  • Reputation effects: How you fight affects how others see you

Conclusion: The Art of Virtual Violence

GURPS combat represents one of gaming's most sophisticated attempts to model realistic fighting while maintaining playability and dramatic tension. You've learned that combat isn't just about rolling dice—it's about making tactical decisions under pressure, managing resources (health, equipment, positioning), and accepting consequences for your choices.

The system rewards smart thinking over raw statistics. A clever player with a weaker character can defeat a stronger but less tactically minded opponent through superior positioning, better equipment choices, and strategic use of maneuvers. This makes every fight a mental challenge as much as a statistical contest.

Essential Combat Principles

  • Initiative matters: Going first often means winning
  • Defense is active: You choose how to avoid attacks
  • Damage accumulates: Shock, major wounds, and low HP can spiral quickly
  • Tactics trump stats: Smart play beats raw power
  • Equipment affects outcomes: Right tools for the right job
  • Environment shapes combat: Use everything around you
  • Consequences persist: Violence has lasting effects
